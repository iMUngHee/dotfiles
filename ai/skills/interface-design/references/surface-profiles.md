# Surface Profiles

Read the shared quality floor, the section of `medium-profiles.md` for the declared medium,
and only the primary profile matching the approved experience.

A profile owns **what the surface is optimizing for**. The medium owns the substrate: units,
capabilities, which states exist, and how the result is inspected. Where a purpose needs a
concrete number that only makes sense in one medium, that number lives in the medium's purpose
calibration, not here.

These are starting points, not specifications. The priorities describe what usually serves that
kind of surface; the content in front of you decides whether they hold. A profile never
overrides an approved contract, a selected artifact, or an accessibility minimum — and a
deliberate departure with a stated reason beats a compliant default.

Read a profile to know what the surface is optimizing for and what it must not become. If a
value here conflicts with what the job actually needs, the job wins; say so and move on.

## Precedence

Highest wins. `medium-profiles.md` carries the same chain.

1. Invariant floor intent plus the declared medium's accessibility mechanism.
2. The approved contract and the selected artifact, within their reviewed scope.
3. The actual job.
4. Purpose × medium calibration.
5. This profile's priorities.
6. The medium's craft defaults.

Layers 4-6 are all starting points. An artifact that contradicts the contract is
`ARTIFACT DRIFT` and stops — never resolved by ranking.

## Dashboard / Admin / Operations

- Job: scan, compare, filter, recover, and repeat actions quickly.
- Density: compact — rows tight, controls tight, containers modest.
- Structure: persistent navigation, a dense scannable list as the centre, and detail that
  arrives transiently. Never hero-led.
- Type: numbers and identifiers use aligned, equal-width figures.
- Motion: none for repeated row/filter/keyboard actions; brief direct feedback only.
- Components: sortable and filterable dense listings where useful, overflow and bulk states,
  empty and error rows, and traversal that stays usable as volume grows.
- Avoid: marketing copy, decorative whitespace, work queues presented as decorative blocks
  instead of a scannable list, color-only priority, and repeated-action animation.

## Form-Heavy Flow

- Job: complete data entry with confidence, recovery, and minimal rework.
- Density: moderate; group fields into a comfortably readable column with a steady vertical
  rhythm.
- Structure: explicit labels, progressive sections for long flows, stable action placement,
  and review/save visibility.
- Motion: brief validation or section feedback; none while typing.
- Components: label, helper, invalid, disabled, loading, readonly, required/optional, dirty,
  and saved states as applicable.
- Avoid: placeholder-only labels, hidden errors, unexplained disabled submit, irreversible
  dead ends, and avoidable late validation.

## Data Visualization

- Job: compare, detect patterns, and trust the data.
- Density: preserve definitions, labels, legends, and annotations before decorative whitespace.
- Structure: metric definition, timeframe, source/update status, axes, legend, and empty/error
  state belong to the chart.
- Type: aligned, equal-width figures for numbers.
- Color: categorical palettes differ in hue **and** luminance; sequential palettes use ordered
  lightness. Where the palette is not the author's to control, the medium says so and supplies
  the substitute — the floor's "never color-only" still holds.
- Motion: only when it preserves object constancy through filtering or time changes.
- Avoid: unlabelled axes, decorative gradients, false depth, cropped legends, and unlabeled
  totals.

## Landing / Marketing

- Job: understand the offer, trust it, and choose the next action.
- Density: low to moderate; the **first contact unit** — whatever the medium shows before the
  reader acts — carries product/brand signal, value, proof or product evidence, and one primary
  action.
- Structure: derive a coherent macrostructure from the offer and audience; do not default to
  interchangeable section stacks.
- Motion: low-frequency and brand/product-serving; content survives with motion removed.
- Components: navigation, primary action, product media, proof, objections, and pricing or next
  step as applicable.
- Avoid: vague claims, stock-like visuals, headline copy trapped in generic containers, hidden
  product evidence, and competing primary actions.

## Content / Editorial

- Job: consume, evaluate, and navigate content.
- Density: reading-first; main measure 60-80 characters.
- Structure: article body, useful table of contents, media and captions, and related navigation
  driven by content.
- Motion: minimal and never interrupting reading flow.
- Components: headings, links, media, code, callouts, footnotes, and search/no-result states
  when applicable.
- Avoid: operational density, decorative widgets, low-contrast text, broken measure, and
  reading-interrupting animation.

## Game / Expressive Tool

- Job: understand state, feel feedback, and enjoy interaction.
- Density: state-first; primary state remains visible and secondary controls may collapse.
- Structure: the play/work area dominates; status, inventory, score, timers, and mode controls
  stay predictable.
- Type: counters and labels remain legible under motion; expressive display type stays out of
  high-frequency state reading.
- Motion: direct feedback may be richer, but controls keep answering immediately; celebration
  is low-frequency and reducible.
- Components: start, pause, reset, mode, score/state, and invalid action.
- Avoid: ambiguous state, hidden controls, inaccessible key actions, repeated-input animation,
  and effects that cover important state.

## Product App / Workflow

- Job: complete recurring tasks, retain context, and recover safely.
- Density: moderate; work-list rows and container padding sized for sustained repetition.
- Structure: persistent navigation, a clear current object, predictable detail regions, bounded
  interruptions, and recoverable inline editing.
- Motion: brief navigation/detail feedback; avoid animation on repeated power-user flows.
- Components: navigation, action grouping, listing, detail, entry, bounded interruption,
  transient notice, empty/loading/error/conflict, and undo/recovery.
- Avoid: decorative layout that slows repetition, hidden state changes, unrecoverable actions,
  and feature blocks replacing workflows.

## Presenting

- Job: present one idea per unit, legibly at the audience's distance, with speaker support.
- Density: one primary idea per unit and generous breathing room.
- Structure: section dividers, and a title plus one supporting visual or point block.
- Color: one reserved emphasis accent.
- Motion: builds only to pace a single idea.
- Avoid: dense paragraphs and document-like units.

Canvas, unit scale, distance floors, masters, speaker-note support, and advance behaviour are
medium concerns — see the Deck section of `medium-profiles.md`. This profile applies wherever
the job is presenting, including a presentation delivered on another medium.
