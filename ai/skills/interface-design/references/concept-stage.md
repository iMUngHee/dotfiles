# Interface Concept Stage

Use this method after the Experience gate when no applicable visual reference exists, when a redesign materially changes direction, or when `study` is requested. It adapts structural exploration and anti-generic critique without importing a theme or page catalog.

## Candidate Positions

Draft two or three genuinely different readings of the same approved job:

- **Safe read** — the expected competent direction. It anchors comparison but is not the automatic winner.
- **Sharp read** — push one job-relevant dimension such as density rhythm, type voice, palette character, or spatial behavior far enough to be memorable while every floor holds.
- **Borrowed read** — name another domain with a structurally similar job and borrow one relationship from it. Borrow structure, not costume.

If swapping palettes makes two candidates interchangeable, they are one idea in different coats. Redraft.

## Candidate Record

Each candidate states:

- Position label and one-sentence concept.
- Personality and palette role/character.
- Type voice.
- Macrostructure: the screen-level relationship among navigation, primary work/content, context, actions, and feedback.
- Design tension: one dominant rule plus one counterpoint.
- Signature moment: one memorable, job-serving, low-frequency element or justified `N/A`.
- One convention it changes and why the job tolerates it.
- Job closure: how the requested action, success, and recovery stay obvious.

Macrostructure is a coherent bundle, not a catalog label. It must arise from the approved IA, flow, screen inventory, content priority, and surface profile. Never default a workflow surface to landing-page sections or a dense tool to decorative cards.

## Selection

- Owner-taste surface: render all candidates as variants of one representative screen using the same realistic content and markup. Let the owner pick.
- External product surface: choose the sharpest survivor that best serves the job, not merely the most unusual candidate.
- Mixed, stakeholder-not-user, or unclear: gate.
- Rendering unavailable: say so and gate on labeled text concepts. Never silently treat an unrendered direction as selected.

Open the selected direction with:

```text
Art direction: <concept> — <why it fits the product, audience, and job>
```

## Non-Production Specimen

- Use one representative screen per candidate, realistic content, and the same information/state scope.
- Vary tokens, type voice, density, composition, and component expression; do not build different products.
- Include desktop and the relevant narrow/fixed canvas only when it affects the decision.
- Label every artifact `non-production interface specimen`.
- Do not wire APIs, persistence, production routing, or durable application state.

## Redesign Method

1. Capture current content, product identity, accepted workflows, states, constraints, and implementation boundaries.
2. Keep one candidate as `keep and refine` when visual direction changes materially.
3. Change structure, rhythm, hierarchy, and component voice without silently changing approved experience scope.
4. Emit `EXPERIENCE DELTA REQUIRED` for any desired job, IA, state, recovery, or microcopy change.
5. Show what remains, what changes, and why the new direction better serves the job.

## Study Method

1. Inspect the supplied reference at its real viewport and meaningful states when possible.
2. Diagnose structure: hierarchy, macrostructure, density, rhythm, type roles, color roles, component voice, imagery, motion, and responsive transformation.
3. Separate transferable principles from brand-specific costume and content.
4. Map each proposed principle to the target job and existing product constraints.
5. Present the diagnosis for acceptance before it becomes a candidate or contract delta.

## Anti-Generic Critique

Before selection or handoff, answer:

- Can the idea be stated without naming colors or fonts?
- Do candidates differ structurally, not only cosmetically?
- Is hierarchy a consequence of the job and content priority?
- Does the direction contain one controlled tension rather than evenly distributed styling?
- Is the signature moment visible and useful, or is `N/A` justified?
- Could this exact interface belong to an unrelated product after swapping logo and copy?
- Are large type, gradients, pills, cards, shadows, and motion used for a named reason rather than as defaults?

Any unsupported answer is a design gap, not a request to add more decoration.
