# Reference Study

Before drawing candidates, look at how this problem has already been solved well. The point
is not to copy a look; it is to reach the candidate stage holding real structural material
instead of defaults.

The best reference is source. A live product's markup and CSS carry more than a screenshot,
and far more than an adjective.

## Choosing what to study

Pick products that share the **job**, not the industry or the aesthetic. A surface for
reading long Korean essays has more to learn from a well-set book reader than from a
competitor blog on the same subject.

Two or three studied properly beats six skimmed. If the user named a reference, start there —
they are telling you something about their taste they may not be able to state directly.

Prefer references you can open and inspect. When only a screenshot exists, say so and treat
the resulting principles as weaker evidence.

## What to extract

Read for structure first, expression second:

- How the screen divides, and what that division says about what matters.
- What the eye hits first, second, third — and what produces that ordering.
- Where density tightens and where it loosens, and how the content justifies each.
- What the type is doing: how many roles, how they differ, which one carries voice.
- How color is allocated — which roles are reserved, what is deliberately absent.
- What holds the composition together: a grid, a rule, a repeated interval, an anchor.
- What this reference does that a generic version of the same product would not.

Then separate transferable from specific. Brand color, logo, and copy voice are costume. The
relationships between elements are structure. Borrow structure.

## What to record

Keep each reference short: what it is, why it is relevant to this job, the two or three
structural moves worth taking, and what to leave behind. A paragraph each.

Name at least one thing the reference does that you will **not** do, and why. A study that
only admires has not been done.

## Feeding the candidates

Every principle carried forward should trace to a reference and to the job it serves. A
candidate that cannot say where its structure came from is a default in disguise.

Do not let one reference dominate every candidate. If all candidates trace to the same
source, the study narrowed exploration instead of widening it — go back and study something
structurally different.

## When no reference exists

Say so rather than inventing one. Then reason from the job: what must the user see, compare,
or act on, and what arrangement serves that? Record it as reasoning, not as a studied
reference.
